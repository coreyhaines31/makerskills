# Rolling out a company brain

Adoption beats announcement. Launching to the whole company on day one creates a pile of unreviewed captures you spend months culling, and people stop trusting answers before the brain has earned any trust.

## Phase 1 — Pilot (2–4 weeks)

- **One workflow**, seeded per "Start narrow" in `vault-config.md`.
- **One or two people** who live in that workflow daily and will actually run `/cb review`.
- Capture everything relevant to the workflow; don't capture outside it yet.
- Hold a weekly review pass. If it slips, fix that before anything else; it's the habit everything else depends on.

## Phase 2 — Prove value

Move on only when all three are true:

| Signal | Bar |
|---|---|
| Queries answered | The pilot group asks it real questions weekly and gets useful answers |
| Review cadence | Weekly review has held for 3+ weeks; the unreviewed queue isn't growing |
| Trust | Answers cite `verified` sources often enough that people stop double-checking |

If a signal misses, narrow rather than widen: fewer dirs, fewer sources, a tighter workflow.

## Phase 3 — Expand

- Add **one** workflow (and its dirs) at a time, with an owner who runs review for it.
- Add people in the order they'll contribute, not by org chart.
- Turn on auto-sync (call transcripts, Slack, CRM) per workflow only after manual capture has proven which content is worth keeping. Auto-sync on day one multiplies the review queue before anyone knows what good looks like.
- Set sensitivity levels before leadership or confidential content arrives, not after.

## Anti-patterns

- **The big launch.** All dirs, all people, all syncs at once. Review can't keep up, so trust never forms.
- **Capture without review.** A growing `unreviewed` queue is the leading indicator of a brain nobody trusts.
- **Measuring pages, not answers.** Page count is vanity. Questions answered without asking a person is the metric.
